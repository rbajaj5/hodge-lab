## Broader support and conditional sector criteria

Benjamin Diamond’s merged PR 264 adds §3B.10 for a smooth sextic fourfold whose equation splits as `F = H(x₀,x₁,x₂,x₃) + B(s,t)` in some linear coordinates. The book places the binary-sign-negative fourth cohomology on a divisor. When the equation also has the prescribed involution with three positive and three negative coordinate directions, it places the entire invariant fourth cohomology on a divisor. The splitting can initially use arbitrary linear coordinates. The stated consequence gives certificates for every odd numerator at every positive pole order on an even sextic of this kind. The argument uses the earlier Wronskian spanning results and finite Fano maps. The PR credits OpenAI Codex assistance. [Pinned theorem](https://github.com/hodge-computer/sextics/blob/e5f46e0758306e8bb51de12e21e261ec1df19e48/docs/03b-weighted-threefolds.md#3b10-sextics-with-a-binary-summand) · [PR 264](https://github.com/hodge-computer/sextics/pull/264)

## Four-plus-two discussion report

Benjamin Diamond’s new #120 post reports that any legal degree-six denominator for the prescribed `q=1` target has the form `D = D_c + λF`, with a nonzero class of `D_c` in a 33-dimensional kernel in the core Jacobian ring. The report allows complex denominators of arbitrary parity. It reduces degree-six certificate existence to finding a core sextic `D_c` and a degree-seven polynomial core field `p` satisfying

\[
p(H)=A D_c,\qquad D_c\operatorname{div}(p)=p(D_c).
\]

The post explicitly leaves this pair open for the prescribed source. It connects the condition to DiamondJM’s core equation and the degree-six case left open by kmehltretter82’s bounded checks, and sharpens kejace’s earlier degree-at-most-five exclusion. [Post and prior-work links](https://github.com/hodge-computer/sextics/discussions/120#discussioncomment-19694056)

The same post reports an obstruction to one sextic denominator serving every odd sextic source. It also reports absolute simplicity of the three-dimensional Prym carrying the binary differential, with geometric rational endomorphism algebra ℚ(√−3), excluding an elliptic quotient for that differential. The evidence described includes finite-field multiplication and Hessian checks, Frobenius calculations and root-ratio tests. The author provides Sage scripts and credits Codex assistance plus Claude-assisted independent recomputation. These remain author-reported mathematical checks in this notebook.
