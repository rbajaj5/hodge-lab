# Which syzygy results apply to the holdout calculation?

Recorded 16 October 2026 (EDT), after the family-115 proof and the separate
family-16 global cube identities. This is a literature assessment and a
proposed computational direction. It adds no exclusion or upstream post.

## The three sources

**David Eisenbud, *The Geometry of Syzygies* (2005).** Chapter 1 develops
polynomial modules, free resolutions, and graded Betti numbers. Appendix A2G
treats determinantal and Fitting ideals; A2H treats Eagon--Northcott complexes.
These are directly relevant tools for a polynomial correction matrix. They
suggest examining its cokernel and the loci where its rank drops, in addition
to seeking bounded-degree relations. A2.60 requires the appropriate grade of
the maximal-minor ideal for the stated resolution; we have not verified that
hypothesis for our matrix. A resolution or a regularity bound for the actual
module would need to be computed or proved, not inferred from the book's
examples. [Source, Chapters 1 and A2G--A2H](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~reiner/REU/REU2019notes/2005_Book_TheGeometryOfSyzygies.pdf).

**Lawrence Ein, Robert Lazarsfeld, David Yang, “A vanishing theorem for
weight-one syzygies” (2016).** Theorem A gives
`K_{p,1}(X,B;L_d)=0` when `B` is p-jet very ample and `L_d=dH+P`, with `H`
ample and `d` sufficiently large, on a smooth complex projective variety.
Applying it here would require identifying the relevant section module and
proving these hypotheses. No such identification is available. In particular,
the paper's weight-one index is not our certificate parameter `q=1`, and
“sufficiently large” supplies no degree-seven stopping rule.
[Source, Theorem A and its setup](https://msp.org/ant/2016/10-9/ant-v10-n9-p05-s.pdf).

**Federico Caucci, Marti Lahoz, “Stability of syzygy bundles on abelian
varieties,” arXiv:2007.08846v3.** The bundle in question is
`M_L = ker(H^0(X,L) tensor O_X -> L)`. Theorem 1.1 gives semistability of
`M_{L^d}` for a polarized abelian variety and `d>=2`; Theorem 1.5 gives a
slope-stability criterion involving intersection numbers on abelian
subvarieties. This is geometric bundle stability, not numerical conditioning.
Our correction matrix has not been identified with this evaluation map on an
abelian variety. Even on an auxiliary elliptic curve, a result about this
bundle would not by itself compute a pushforward to the holdout's target.
[Source, Theorems 1.1 and 1.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.08846v3).

## A concrete translation to our existing equations

The following is our application of ordinary polynomial and field linear
algebra, not an application of either paper's vanishing or stability theorem.
After the checked E1 reductions, the physical coefficient equations are

```text
E(a,z) = R(a) z + b(a) = 0.
```

Here `a` denotes denominator coefficients, `z` the shared numerator
corrections, `R` is linear and `b` quadratic before chart normalization.
Our bounded search finds elements of `ker(R^T)` over the polynomial ring:
`u(a)^T R(a)=0` implies the necessary condition `u(a)^T b(a)=0`.
It does not claim to generate the full module.

Even a full polynomial kernel needs care when parameters are specialized:
evaluated global relations need not span all relations at a rank-drop point.
The [previous note](polynomial-syzygies.md) already records the example

```text
x*z + 1 = 0,  y*z + 1 = 0.
```

Its polynomial left kernel is generated by `(y,-x)`, giving `y-x=0`.
At `(x,y)=(0,0)` that generator evaluates to zero and its consequence holds,
but both original equations become `1=0`. This remains a necessary-versus-
sufficient distinction, not a defect in our exclusion proofs.

For a *fixed* complex parameter value `a`, an exact criterion is

```text
there exists z  <=>  rank R(a) = rank [ R(a) | -b(a) ].
```

This gives a proposed way to organize the search by rank. On the stratum
`rank R=r`, all `(r+1)`-minors of `R` vanish and at least one `r`-minor is
nonzero. Solvability additionally requires all `(r+1)`-minors of the
augmented matrix to vanish. For `r=0`, the condition is simply `R=0, b=0`.
The nonvanishing condition matters: dropping it can retain spurious lower-
rank points such as the example above.

On an open patch with a chosen nonzero `r`-minor, use that block to solve
for selected corrections and substitute into the other equations. Clearing
its determinant is valid on that patch; the zero-determinant branch must
also be handled. This could produce smaller compatibility systems without
enumerating all minors. Whether it saves time on holdout is unknown.

## Next experiment and limits

Rebuild family 16 after its [checked global zeros](../algebra/check_family16_global_cubes.py),
preserving every allowed correction direction. Compare bounded syzygy
elimination with elimination on selected nonzero-minor patches, explicitly
retaining the complementary branches. Then examine family 137. Record exact
identities, timings, failed bounds, and uncovered branches. Neither this
rank-stratified experiment nor a free resolution is executed in this entry.

The existing coverage remains 134/136 fixed-`X0/Y0` extensions. A failed
bounded search is not a vanishing theorem. A compatible numerical rank is
not an exact certificate. A bundle-stability theorem does not establish a
nonzero target Gysin image. These sources guide the next calculation without
changing the evidence for the fifth upstream post.
