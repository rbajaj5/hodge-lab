## Transferring one residue certificate

Benjamin Diamond merged PR 250, whose book changes are attributed to Claude. It adds two results to Chapter 1. For a smooth hypersurface and a pencil of hyperplane sections `t = λs`, the book gives an explicit lift from a certificate over `k(λ)` for the section numerator **s·Aλ** to a certificate for the original numerator **A**. The factor `s` comes from the volume form. For ambient dimension `n ≥ 3` and `1 ≤ q ≤ n−2`, the corollary starts from divisor support for that residue on one smooth section whose parameter is transcendental over the ground field. [Pencil lemma and corollary](https://github.com/hodge-computer/sextics/blob/34993b6d1ad0b0a4b6b4f1c1eb75954e963c2e44/docs/01-certificates.md#pencils-and-specialization)

The specialization theorem starts with a certificate over the function field of a smooth base curve. For a family with regular coefficients and `1 ≤ q ≤ n−1`, it asserts existence over the ground field at every rational base point with smooth hypersurface fiber. The generic primitive may have a pole along that fiber. The exactness lemma used in the proof requires the top-form coefficient to be regular at the generic point of the chosen component, where the base parameter generates the maximal ideal of the discrete valuation ring. Over an irreducible base, the book also gives a way to obtain the generic certificate from one complex point lying on no proper closed subvariety defined over the ground field. These statements concern the class of a single residue. Chapter 3 retains separate hypotheses for a correspondence supporting an entire sector. [Specialization theorem](https://github.com/hodge-computer/sextics/blob/34993b6d1ad0b0a4b6b4f1c1eb75954e963c2e44/docs/01-certificates.md#specialization) · [Chapter 3](https://github.com/hodge-computer/sextics/blob/34993b6d1ad0b0a4b6b4f1c1eb75954e963c2e44/docs/03-geometric-support.md) · [PR 250](https://github.com/hodge-computer/sextics/pull/250)

## Two repairs to the cycle-q2 argument

Chapter 3D now identifies its local bundle of curves as open in the **classical topology** of the relative Hilbert scheme, which it asserts is smooth along the bundle. The moving-class argument uses the algebraic component containing that bundle and cuts it to relative dimension one with the required tangent direction. The revised derivative lemma applies the Abel–Jacobi map to the difference between the moving curve and a fixed reference curve on the same threefold. That difference is homologous to zero. [Chapter 3D](https://github.com/hodge-computer/sextics/blob/34993b6d1ad0b0a4b6b4f1c1eb75954e963c2e44/docs/03d-hodge-loci.md) · [Book revision](https://github.com/hodge-computer/sextics/commit/e7c98644a0e0af3e34c97c0a7b208c0874d3806c)

The upstream book already asserted certificate existence for `cycle-q2` on 11 November. These repairs amend its argument.
