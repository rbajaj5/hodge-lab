# Divisor support for a nontrivial order-three sector

## What changed in Chapter 3D

Benjamin Diamond merged PR 263 on 17 November 2026. The book now allows the surface section and the divisor class to transform under nontrivial characters. Section 3D.1 now specifies that its positive dimension count requires a symmetry moving the holomorphic three-form when both the class and the section are invariant. Section 3D.2 also explains why the Abel–Jacobi derivative integral is independent of the chosen lift when the curve class stays of type (1,1). The source commit is attributed to Claude, and the chapter credits Voisin for the strategy of moving cycles along Hodge loci. [Book revision](https://github.com/hodge-computer/sextics/commit/b3ce58cc06949a19a66eeca6773d48e98a11770c) · [Merged PR 263](https://github.com/hodge-computer/sextics/pull/263)

For a quasismooth sextic threefold in the weighted projective space ℙ(1,1,1,1,2), §3D.6 assumes that its equation is invariant under `g = (1, ω, ω, ω; 1)`, where ω is a primitive cube root of unity. This action fixes the holomorphic three-form. The book states that the sum of the two nontrivial eigenspaces of the third cohomology is supported on a divisor, with Hodge numbers **(0, 63, 63, 0)**. More precisely, for some divisor Z the projector onto that rational sector has an algebraic representative supported on Z × Z. The invariant eigenspace, which contains the holomorphic form, lies outside the theorem. [Order-three sector theorem](https://github.com/hodge-computer/sextics/blob/0cb9d23db548bb2c4c33f20d78e51c34826c00a8/docs/03d-hodge-loci.md#3d6-other-sectors-and-a-symmetry-that-fixes-the-three-form)

For the smooth sextic fourfold application, the equation has both the paired sign on (X₂,Y₂) and `γ = diag(1, ω, 1, ω, ω, 1)` in the book’s coordinate order. The assertion covers the part of the paired-sign-invariant fourth cohomology with no γ-invariants. On smooth Y-even members the supported part of the Y-even primitive cohomology has Hodge numbers **(74, 298, 74)**. For pole parameter 1 ≤ q ≤ 4, the book obtains certificate existence for a Y-odd numerator even in (X₂,Y₂) when each monomial has degree nondivisible by three in (X₁,Y₀,Y₁). The displayed `cycle-q2` numerator has degree six in those three variables. Its previously recorded existence claim uses the different symmetry of §3D.5. [Fourfold application](https://github.com/hodge-computer/sextics/blob/0cb9d23db548bb2c4c33f20d78e51c34826c00a8/docs/03d-hodge-loci.md#3d6-other-sectors-and-a-symmetry-that-fixes-the-three-form) · [Earlier cycle-q2 entry](upstream-monitor-20261111.md)

The chapter reports a seed calculation modulo 109 and a scalar-symmetry check for 40 invariant directions on a 63-dimensional space. We inspected the new verifier source. These computations are upstream’s supporting evidence. ## What the fourfold dimension count rules out

Chapter 4 considers a smooth surface cut out on a smooth even sextic fourfold by two equations of specified Y-parity. For each degree pair 1 ≤ e₁ ≤ e₂ ≤ 4, it counts motions of the surface inside the fixed fourfold and subtracts the dimension of its invariant holomorphic two-forms. The largest difference is **−1**, for two linear equations of the same parity, with two motions and three forms. At a transverse Hodge locus the curve-family construction in §4.4 needs a difference of at least **2**. Excess-dimensional loci, higher degrees, other surfaces and directly constructed curve families remain outside the count. [Fourfold dimension count](https://github.com/hodge-computer/sextics/blob/0cb9d23db548bb2c4c33f20d78e51c34826c00a8/docs/04-known-locus-and-frontier.md#a-dimension-count-for-surface-sections-of-the-fourfold) · [Dimension verifier](https://github.com/hodge-computer/sextics/blob/0cb9d23db548bb2c4c33f20d78e51c34826c00a8/scripts/verify-extension-dimensions.py)
